Generated by `scripts/report_retrieval.py`. Do not edit by hand.

The labels come from the books: when a proof or solution cites a numbered result, that statement is the
query and the cited result is the right answer. A test split of whole modules was run once, after the
settings were chosen on the other split.

306 questions, 378 cited results. Each question hides its own statement and solution or proof.

## Where it's weak

- Results from another module (72 questions): recall@10 is 0.150 for search alone and 0.062 once position in the book is added.
- Exercises make up only 44 of the questions; the other 262 are statements of results.
- A citation is only one right answer. Other useful passages count as misses, so these are lower bounds.
- Position in the book only exists for exercises from the book, not for a student's own question.

## The leak

If each exercise's own solution weren't hidden, search would return it first for 15% of 458 exercises and in the top 5 for 31%.

## Cross-volume links

None of the books' numbered references cross volumes, so these links come from earlier volumes' indexes:
a term in a statement leads to the definition on the page the index gives.

- Volume 2: 172 of 300 worked exercises get a link, from 262 usable terms.
- Volume 3: 106 of 158 worked exercises get a link, from 443 usable terms.

| Method | Statement really uses the linked concept | 95% CI |

Judged by Claude from a shuffled sample with the method hidden, not yet by a person. A correct link isn't necessarily the one a student is missing: many point at basics such as what a matrix is.
